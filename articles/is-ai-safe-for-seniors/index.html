Is AI safe for seniors?

It can be, with good habits. No AI tool is risk-free, and none can promise to spot a scam for you. What helps most is knowing the limits and keeping a few simple routines.

What AI is good at

Tools like ChatGPT can explain a topic in plain language, help you word an email, suggest questions to ask a doctor's office or a bank, or walk you through a setting on your phone. Think of it as a patient helper that is always available.

Where it can let you down

  • It can be wrong. An AI answer can sound confident and still contain mistakes, especially about recent events, prices, hours, laws or health.
  • It does not know you. It cannot see your accounts and cannot tell whether a message you received is real.
  • It is not a professional. For medical, legal or money decisions, use it to prepare your questions, then ask a qualified person.

What not to share

Treat a chat like a conversation in a public place. Do not type passwords, banking details, card numbers, your social insurance number or other identity numbers. Check the app's privacy settings, including whether your chats can be used to improve the service.

Five habits that keep you in control

  1. Pause before you act on any message that asks for money, a code or a password, whoever it seems to come from.
  2. Contact the company or person yourself, using a phone number or website you already trust, not the one in the message.
  3. Check anything important in a second place before relying on it.
  4. Keep your software up to date and use a different password for each account.
  5. Ask someone you trust when something feels off. There is no such thing as a silly question.
